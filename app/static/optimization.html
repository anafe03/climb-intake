Which one we use: gpt-5. It caught all 21 tickets that needed escalating and made the fewest mistakes. Its one false alarm is the "we have not had a data breach" ticket, where the keyword backup flags it on purpose. Its one urgency miss was on a borderline ticket, where high and critical are both fair answers.

Why not the cheapest: nano missed 2 of the 21 escalations, and read 4 clearly critical tickets as less urgent, including the checkout charging customers twice. A critical ticket read as less urgent waits in a normal queue instead of paging someone.

A cheaper setup we tested: use the cheap model (mini) by default, and only use gpt-5 when a ticket looks risky. The free keyword list decides which is which before any model reads it. If it spots security, legal or senior-person words, or urgent wording, gpt-5 reads the ticket. Otherwise mini does. Each ticket is read once, by one model. About half the tickets went to each, and mini costs about a fifth as much, so the bill fell 38%. The price: on the 33 main test tickets it made one false escalation and one urgency call too low that gpt-5 on its own did not.

Why not ask mini first, then gpt-5 if it looks unsure? We tried that too. It costs 29% more, not less, because every risky ticket gets paid for twice: once by mini, then again by gpt-5. And the risky tickets are the expensive ones, because they take the most thinking.

Why we did not switch: 38% of about $9 is about $3.50 a month at 1,000 tickets. At 50,000 tickets a month it is about $170 a month. That is roughly where I would start to consider it. The 38% was measured; the 50,000 is my judgement call, not a measurement.
