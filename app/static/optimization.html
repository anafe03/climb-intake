Recommendation

Run gpt-5 at low reasoning effort. Do not optimise this yet.

It is the only configuration measured with no missed escalations, no false escalations and no urgency under-calls on the gold set. It costs 0.9¢ a ticket — about $9 per thousand, or roughly what one support hour costs per forty thousand tickets.

Two cheaper options are real and measured, and both are below. Neither is worth taking at this volume: the saving is a few dollars a month and the cost is a class of error you then have to explain. Revisit at about 50,000 tickets a month, where the 38% saving becomes real money and is worth the extra failure mode.

Category accuracy is not in that table on purpose. Every model scores 100% on it, including the one disqualified below. A number that cannot separate a frontier model from the cheapest one is measuring the task, not the model, so it does not belong in a decision table. Strict category accuracy does separate the two modes — 100% on the model path against 82% rules-only — and that is in the scorecards.

Why gpt-5-nano is disqualified at 26× cheaper It read three critical tickets as high, one of them a provided sample: the checkout flow double-charging customers. Those tickets sit. It scores 100% on category and 100% on escalation recall while doing it, which is the whole argument for reporting urgency by direction — an accuracy score calls nano a bargain.
And why the free row is not the answer either Keyword rules alone score 100% on the gold set and then miss three escalations out of twelve on the adversarial set — a leak described as "probably nothing", credentials written cr3ds, and the same incident in Spanish. Free is not cheap if it misses one.

I built the cascade. The obvious version loses money.

Worth showing because the arithmetic looks fine right up until you measure it. Draft every ticket on gpt-5-mini, then re-read on gpt-5 when the draft escalates, calls it high or critical, or is unsure. 58% got re-read, so the naive sum says about 22% saved.

Why the draft version loses: adverse selection The tickets a cascade chooses to re-read are, by construction, the hard ones — they escalate, or they are urgent. A hard ticket costs nearly double the average on the expensive model — 1.73¢ against a 0.91¢ average — so the draft becomes pure overhead on most of the spend and the saving never arrives. It is invisible if you reason about an average cost per ticket instead of pricing each one.

The version that works does the triage for free. The keyword layer already reads every ticket — it is the guardrail. Let it choose the reader instead of paying a model to choose: anything that trips an escalation pattern, or reads as high urgency on keywords alone, goes to gpt-5; everything else goes to gpt-5-mini. One model call either way. 17 of 33 tickets took the expensive path and the bill fell 38%.

What it costs is mini's error profile on the half it handles: one false escalation, the SOC 2 document request again, and one low-versus-medium urgency disagreement. Nothing missed and no critical ticket under-called, which are the two results that would rule it out. Turn it on with LLM_CASCADE=1 CASCADE_TRIAGE=rules.
