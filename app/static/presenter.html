Same ticket, same answer?

2.5 min
DoOpen Explanations, then Keeping it consistent. Point at the five-runs table, then the "where the keyword check can change the AI's answer" table.

"This was the biggest question last time: how do you control consistency? The keyword check and the routing table are rules, so they always give the same answer. The AI might not, so that is the part I control and the part I measured."

4 · How it's built API + terminal

8

It's a service, not a web page

2 min

Have a terminal ready beside the browser. The point: the page is one client. Everything it does is an endpoint your other systems can call.

Classify one ticketThe same call the page makes.
curl -s localhost:8080/tickets \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"text":"A contractor we released Friday still has an active token against our workspace."}' \
  | jq '{category:.extraction.category, urgency:.extraction.urgency,
         escalate:.extraction.escalate, why:.extraction.escalation_reason_text,
         queue:.queue, also:.escalation_queue}'
Ask it why, in plain textFor a person reading a ticket comment.
ID=$(curl -s 'localhost:8080/tickets?limit=1' | jq -r '.[0].id')
curl -s localhost:8080/tickets/$ID/explain

"Same decision, three shapes: the page, data for a system, and plain text for a person. The plain text is what you'd paste into a Slack thread."

Batch uploadCSV, JSON, JSONL or blank-line-separated text.
printf 'id,text\n5001,"Export is broken and reporting is due Friday."\n' > /tmp/t.csv
curl -s -X POST localhost:8080/tickets/upload -F 'file=@/tmp/t.csv' | jq '.count'
Then open the API tabThe docs are generated from the code, so they can't go stale.

In the 15-minute cut: the first two commands only.

If asked about duplicates: send the same ticket twice with an external_id: the same decision comes back. Your systems retry; without this a retry puts a duplicate on the escalation desk.

Questions you should expect

Is it consistent, or is it an AI?

Both, on purpose. The AI reads. Rules decide anything that must not vary: the escalation floor, where a ticket goes, duplicates, the audit. I ran your ten tickets five times each: the type, escalation, queue and company never changed.

Who assigns the confidence score?

With the AI, the AI, following a written scale so 0.85 means the same thing every time. When the AI is down, fixed numbers from the keyword check. The ticket says which ran.

Why guess the customer at all?

Nine of ten tickets name nobody, and "unknown" helps no one. It guesses, scores it, and shows the evidence, but never writes a guess into the name field. In production this comes from the login.

Why can't the fallback find a company name anywhere in a sentence?

The keyword fallback uses word patterns, and those were the best choice here. Patterns cannot recognise every name: there is no list of every company to check against, so "Meridian Health" in the middle of a sentence looks like any other words. A named entity recognition model could spot it, and that is the next step. From a business side it is not the riskiest part: a missed name slows a reply, a missed escalation costs real money. The AI reads names anywhere; this only matters when it is down.

Why doesn't a full outage escalate?

Escalation is about topic, not how bad it is. An outage is critical and goes to on-call. A CEO thank-you note escalates at low urgency. Mixing them would flood the escalation desk.

What's weakest?

I wrote 23 of the 33 main test tickets. If I already knew a way it breaks, I would have fixed it, so the hardest failures to test for are the ones I haven't thought of. That's why the trick tickets exist, and why the next step is real tickets labelled by other people.
